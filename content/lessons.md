## 1. Dome of the Rock — Qubbat al-Sakhra (قبة الصخرة)
- REFS: `dome-ext/temple-mount-V4-178.jpg`, `dome-int/arches.jpg`, `dome-int/sidepart-1.jpg`, `dome-int/2018-01.jpg`, `maps/plan-1890.jpg` (Platform).
- SEE: golden ribbed dome over a blue-tiled octagon; white marble base below, cobalt/turquoise
  geometric tiles above with a white calligraphy band; 16-window drum; inside, two ambulatories ring
  the bare Rock under a gilt dome, mosaics of plants/crowns with no figures, ring chandeliers.
- BUILD: 691–692 CE, Abd al-Malik; outer octagon ~20 m/side, walls 11 m (marble 4.9 + tile 6.1);
  dome Ø20.2 m gold-plated; 4 doors cardinal; 16+36 windows; inner ring 4 piers+12 cols.
- HISTORY: the Rock (al-Sakhra) — from here the Prophet ﷺ ascended in the Miʿraj (Qur'an 17:1,
  Sūrat al-Isrāʾ); first qibla direction faced this sanctuary; Umar (15 AH/636) cleared and prayed here;
  240 m Kufic band (72 AH) among earliest Qur'anic inscriptions; Saladin restored after 1187.
- LESSON: why a shrine, not a prayer hall? → double ambulatory = circumambulation path. Ask: what
  do the mosaics show — and what do they deliberately avoid?

## 2. Dome of the Chain — Qubbat al-Silsila (قبة السلسلة)
- REFS: `chain/chain-dome.jpg`, `chain/chain-01.jpg`, `chain/chain-interior-2013.jpg`, root `chain.jpg`.
- SEE: small open pavilion — 11 outer + 6 inner columns, no walls, grey hemisphere on a tiled
  hexagonal drum; mihrab niche on the qibla side.
- BUILD: Umayyad (possibly Abd al-Malik's model/treasury prototype), Ø14 m, 17 reused marble columns;
  Suleiman tiles 1561. Third-largest dome on the Haram.
- HISTORY: Judgment-Day scales (mawāzīn) tradition — a chain of justice hung here; Bay'ah and
  scholarly sittings held beside the Dome. Open form = place of gathering and counsel.
- LESSON: compare with the great Dome — same language, miniature scale. Spot the mihrab: which way is qibla?

## 3. Dome of the Ascension — Qubbat al-Miʿraj (قبة المعراج)
- REFS: GAP — fetch 2+ views before lookdev (`chain/` neighbours show its terrace context).
- SEE: small closed octagon, white stone dome with a crown-cupola; 30 clustered columns sealed
  with marble slabs; door north, mihrab south.
- BUILD: Ayyubid/Crusader-era fabric, Ø5–6 m; rebuilt/restored under Ottomans; white stone plates.
- HISTORY: marks the Miʿraj ascent (Qur'an 17:1; Sahih al-Bukhari/Muslim: the night journey from
  the "Farthest Mosque"); pilgrims' station for duʿaʾ recalling the Prophet's ﷺ ascent.
- LESSON: Isra' vs Miʿraj — horizontal journey then vertical ascent. Which surah opens with it?

## 4. Dome of the Prophet — Qubbat an-Nabi (قبة النبي)
- REFS: GAP — fetch 2+ views (terrace NW cluster).
- SEE: open octagonal pavilion on 8 grey marble columns with red-black-white ablaq arches,
  lead dome with lantern finial; low stone seat-wall, mihrab slab.
- BUILD: 1539 Muhammad Bey, restored 1603–21; Ø3.5–4 m; nightly oil lamp endowed (Haseki Sultan waqf).
- HISTORY: commemorates the Prophet's ﷺ prayer with the prophets on Laylat al-Isrāʾ (Tafsir of 17:1);
  the endowed lamp = continuous hospitality of light at the sanctuary.
- LESSON: ablaq spotting — count the alternating stones. Why keep a lamp burning all night?

## 5. Qibli Mosque — al-Jāmiʿ al-Aqṣā (الجامع الأقصى)
- REFS: `qibli/facade-frontal-04.jpg`, `qibli/facade-section.jpg`, root `qibli-ne.jpg`, `qibli-dome.jpg`, `maps/wilson-buraq.png` (column grid).
- SEE: 7-arched limestone facade (centre taller), silver-grey lead dome over the southern bay,
  flat lead roofs; inside, 7 aisles of marble columns, red patterned carpets, brass chandeliers,
  stained-glass glow, painted wood ceiling.
- BUILD: 83×56 m, 11 m walls; 7 aisles (Fatimid al-Zahir 1034–36, from 15); 45 columns
  (33 marble + 12 stone); dome r~6.5 m, apex ~23 m, 16-drum windows; 121 windows; concrete dome
  1969 re-leaded 1983.
- HISTORY: "al-Masjid al-Aqṣā" of Qur'an 17:1; second mosque on earth after the Haram of Makkah
  (40 years after — Sahih Muslim); first qibla (until 2 AH); Umar's wooden mosque 638; Umayyad,
  Fatimid, Crusader (palace/church), Saladin's liberation 1187 re-consecration; Nur al-Din minbar
  installed 1187; 1969 arson and reconstruction.
- LESSON: walk the nave — 7 doors, 7 aisles. Find the dome bay: why does it sit near the qibla wall?

## 6. Mihrab & Minbar (محراب ومنبر صلاح الدين)
- REFS: root `qibli-interior.jpg` (NEEDS mihrab/minbar closeups — fetch).
- SEE: marble-framed niche with mosaic conch on the south axis; to its right the tall wooden
  minbar — stepped pulpit with interlocked inlay, pointed canopy door.
- BUILD: Salah al-Din mihrab + Crusader-spolia columns; minbar commissioned Aleppo 1168–69
  (Nur al-Din), ~6 m high, 16,500 interlocked pieces, no nails; burned 1969, rebuilt Jordan 2003–07.
- HISTORY: minbar = symbol of the liberation vow fulfilled — built before victory, installed after;
  khatib's weekly reminder of Aqsa in hearts. Direction of prayer = unity of the ummah.
- LESSON: no-nails joinery — how do 16,500 pieces hold? What does the minbar's journey (Aleppo→Jerusalem) teach about intention preceding victory?

## 7. Al-Kas Fountain — al-Kāʾs (الكأس)
- REFS: `maps/wilson-buraq.png` ("Al Kas / The Cup"); GAP — fetch closeup.
- SEE: great circular basin 8–10 m with central fountain column, green iron fence, steel taps,
  low stone seats; "the Cup" on old plans.
- BUILD: Umayyad 709, enlarged 1327–28 (Tankiz); cistern-fed, now mains; ablution taps ring.
- HISTORY: wuduʾ station — purity before prayer (Qur'an 5:6); Tankiz's Mamluk endowments served
  worshippers and travellers; water endowment (sadaqat al-māʾ) as worship.
- LESSON: trace the ritual — taps → seats → mosque door. Why place the fountain on the axis between Dome and Qibli?

## 8. Sabil Qaitbay (سبيل قايتباي)
- REFS: `sabil/kait-bey-626.jpg`, `sabil/kait-bey-2117.jpg`, root `sabil.jpg`.
- SEE: three-tiered >13 m fountain-house — square ablaq base, round drum, pointed arabesque dome
  with bronze crescent; grilled windows, inscription bands, stairs and bench.
- BUILD: 1455 Inal, rebuilt 1482 Qaytbay, restored 1882–83 Abdul Hamid II; only Mamluk dome
  outside Cairo with E–W crescent; star-strap interior vaulting.
- HISTORY: Mamluk sultans' rivalry in charity — free water on the Haram; inscriptions pair
  Qur'an with the builder's name, a reminder that works outlive rulers.
- LESSON: read the tiers — square earth → circle heaven → pointed aspiration. Find the crescent: why E–W?

## 9. Sabil Qasim Pasha / Naranj (سبيل قاسم باشا)
- REFS: root `fountains.jpg` (Qasim + Qaitbay + Naranj pool together).
- SEE: octagonal kiosk (3.5 m), domed, marble paving, 16 faucets, green-pillared wooden canopy,
  shallow Naranj pool beside it.
- BUILD: 1526–27 Qasim Pasha — FIRST Ottoman building on the Haram; lead dome (1920s) → stone (1998).
- HISTORY: Ottoman entry in stone — continuity of care from Mamluks; canopy + benches = rest
  for students of the nearby madrasas (Ashrafiyya/Uthmaniyya).
- LESSON: first of an era in a small building — what changed, what stayed? Count the faucets and sides.

## 10. Minarets (المآذن الأربع)
- REFS: `minaret/fakhriyya-museum.jpg`, `minaret/asbat.jpg`, `minaret/gate-tribes.jpg`,
  root `minaret-silsila.jpg` + `minaret-fakhriyya.jpg`.
- SEE: four square Syrian-type stone towers — Ghawanima (tallest, 38.5 m, decorated),
  Silsila (35 m, balcony on columns), Fakhriyya (shortest, 23 m, on the wall), Asbat
  (slender Ottoman cylinder) — each: shaft → muqarnas balcony → lantern → lead dome + crescent.
- BUILD: Mamluk 1278–1367 (Lajin, Tankiz, Baybars/Qalawun eras), Asbat crown rebuilt post-1927 quake.
- HISTORY: the adhan from al-Aqsa — first raised on the Silsila minaret; minarets oriented to no
  east side (cemetery/Olives) — the call faces the living city. Bilal's legacy in every tower.
- LESSON: match each silhouette to its name. Why do all four differ — what does each dynasty's crown say?

## 11. Gates & Walls (الأبواب والأسوار)
- REFS: `maps/plan-1890.jpg` (gates A–Z), `sabil/cotton-gate.jpg`, `minaret/gate-tribes.jpg`, `misc/platform-portal.jpg`.
- SEE: Herodian bossed ashlars below (2–13 m blocks, 5–100 t), smaller later courses above with
  crenellation; portals: Qattanin's grand ablaq + muqarnas recess (1336), Asbat's chamfered arch,
  Maghariba's ramp-bridge, Rahma's sealed double hall.
- BUILD: 19 BCE Herod expansion (10,000 workers); 12 gates (11 open); walls 19 m exposed / 32 m total.
- HISTORY: each gate a sermon — Silsila (Chain/judgment), Qattanin (scholars' market),
  Maghariba (North-African guardians, quarter demolished 1967), Rahma (Mercy, sealed, eschatology).
  Umar entered by the east; Saladin by the same hope.
- LESSON: pick a gate on the 1890 plan, find its letter, tell its story. Which gate is the only non-Muslim entry today — and why does that matter?

## 12. Olive Gardens & Courtyards (الزيتون والصحون)
- REFS: `aerial/aerial-silwan-kidron.jpg` (gardens E side); GAP — olive closeup.
- SEE: informal olive rows on grass/dirt east + north, grey-green crowns, gnarled trunks;
  cypress sentinels at edges; vast limestone sahn between monuments, prayer mats outdoors.
- BUILD: 60+ olives 3–6 m, spacing 6–10 m; Saharan dust-grey leaf `#6B7F5E`, silver underside.
- HISTORY: "By the fig and the olive" (Qur'an 95:1) — oath by this land; olives = rootedness (sumud),
  harvest as communal rite; courtyards hold Eid crowds and night vigils (iʿtikaf).
- LESSON: stand in the east garden at dusk — Dome gold ahead, Olives behind. What does an oath "by the olive" imply about this soil?

## Events thread (weave through stops)
- **Isrāʾ & Miʿrāj** (stops 1,3,4): 17:1; lesson spine of the whole tour.
- **First qibla → Kaaba turn** (2 AH; stop 5): unity of direction; face the mihrab, feel the axis.
- **Umar's covenant 15 AH** (stops 1,5,11): protection of life, property, churches — read at a gate.
- **Saladin 1187** (stops 5,6): minbar's vow fulfilled; purification of the sanctuary.
- **1969 arson → reconstruction** (stops 5,6): loss and renewal; the rebuilt minbar's 16,500 pieces.
- **Living sanctuary**: students, lamps, water, olives — endowment (waqf) as the engine across all stops.
